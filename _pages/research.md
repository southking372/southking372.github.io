## Physiological Sensing

My current Ph.D. research focuses on **physiological sensing**. I am interested in sensing and computational methods that can capture physiological states continuously, unobtrusively, and reliably in real-world settings.

My current interests include:

- wearable and non-invasive physiological sensing;
- representation learning for physiological signals;
- robust and personalized inference under inter-subject and real-world variability;
- continuous health monitoring and human-centered sensing systems.

A long-term goal of my work is to connect **sensors, physiological signals, and intelligent models** into practical systems that can provide meaningful and trustworthy health information outside traditional clinical settings.

## Research Foundations

### PPG and Cuffless Blood Pressure Monitoring

My previous work investigated non-invasive blood pressure estimation from photoplethysmography (PPG), including self-supervised learning, diffusion models, differential physiological features, and transfer-learning-based approaches. This work provides an important foundation for my broader interest in physiological sensing and continuous health monitoring.

### Wearable Sensing

I have also worked on flexible wearable fiber sensors for limb motion recognition and physiological monitoring, which motivated my interest in integrating sensing hardware with data-driven physiological inference.

### Biomedical AI

My collaborative work in biomedical AI includes breast ultrasound analysis and computational pathology, including efficient deep learning models for lesion detection/segmentation and diffusion models for virtual pathological staining.
